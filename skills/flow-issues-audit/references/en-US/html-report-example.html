Scenario by scenario

Welcome with an A/B test

#1001 · launched by the "customer registered" event · 3 problems + 1 suggestion
High
Problems
The A/B test is built on a random split instead of the A/B-test block. A 50/50 split block: one branch is SMS, the other email.
Rebuild it on the A/B-test block — it keeps a customer in one variant. A random split sends the same customer into different variants on repeat entries and distorts the comparison.
Subscription to the channel being sent isn't checked before sending. Before the SMS branch there is no subscription check at all; before the email only the brand-level subscription is checked.
Check the subscription for the channel actually being sent (and for the email's topic, if it has one): SMS before the SMS step, email before the email step. A brand-level subscription is not consent for the channel.
Contact validity isn't checked. There is no contact check before the send steps.
Add "valid email" before the email step and "valid phone" before the SMS step — otherwise bounces grow and sender reputation suffers.
Suggestions (optional)
The segment filter sits after the start and after a one-day wait. A condition right after the 1-day wait (customers outside the segment leave the scenario).
If the filter is purely static, move it into the launch conditions so customers outside the audience never enter and never wait a day for nothing (saves load). Keep it as a separate step if you need to see the drop-off in the funnel. A load-vs-clarity trade-off, not a defect.
Check manually
Whether the emails have a subscription topic — that decides whether to check the topic or the channel.

Scheduled reactivation

#1004 · launched on a schedule
OK
✓ No serious problems found.
Suggestions (optional)
The checks are separate steps after the start. Several filter conditions in a row after the schedule start block.
Some of the checks could move into the schedule's launch conditions, cutting the audience before entry and lowering load. If the steps are kept separate on purpose — to see where the audience drops off — that is fine, leave them. A load-vs-clarity trade-off.
Check manually
Whether the email has a subscription topic — that decides the level of the subscription check (topic or channel).

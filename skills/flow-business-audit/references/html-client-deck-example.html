01 "Abandoned cart" Fix

The highest-earning flow loses subscribers five times faster than the rest

0.48% Unsubscribe % (Email) — against 0.12% for the projectAugust 2026, 14,550 deliveries; from the project's reporting data
Unsubscribe % (Email) by month, September 2025 — August 2026 · from the project's reporting data

The flow earns more than any other — $1,185,769.20 in August — and produces 70 of the project's 114 unsubscribes on 15% of its deliveries. A year ago the rate was half that: 0.24%. Over the same year the project grew by 40%, this flow doubled.

▸Where exactly it is lost

Of the 70 unsubscribes, 58 come from two emails out of five: the third (34) and the fifth (24). The other three emails together produce 12.

August 2026, flow report by message; the same month and the same goal as the figure on the slide.

▸What is in the flow

Five sends on one path with pauses of 1 hour, 1 day, 1 day, 2 days and 2 days. One check before the first email — "Subscribed and valid" — and no repeat check after the pauses. The flow has no re-entry limit.

Flow structure and executions for the last 30 days (August 18 — September 17), not for August: 12,410 executions entered, 9,880 passed the check, 9,865 reached the fifth email.

▸What to do

Lengthen the pauses before the third and the fifth email from one day to two and repeat the "Subscribed and valid" check after every pause — for whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show the result: the unsubscribe % of the third and the fifth email over the next full month against 0.48% now.

The hypothesis this tests: the unsubscribes come from the emails that arrive soonest after the previous one — the third and the fifth sit behind one-day pauses. If the unsubscribes spread evenly across the series, the cause is not the pauses.

The threshold this report applies is 2%. These three account for 9% of the project's sends and 65% of all bounces. Every bounce is an email to an address that does not exist, and mailbox providers count them across the domain as a whole.

▸Where exactly it breaks

In "Welcome series", 369 of the 421 bounces are on the first email (4.95% of its sends), 52 on the second (1.06%). Addresses fail at the first contact, not over the course of the series.

August 2026, flow report by message.

▸What is in the flow

"Welcome series": the trigger is the event "subscription status changed", the path to the first email is a 1-hour pause → the condition "Subscribed, valid, no orders" → "Send Welcome 1". The only address check in the flow is "email exists and is invalid = no", inside that condition: it fires on the invalid flag, that is, on an address that has already bounced.

Structure and executions for August 18 — September 17: 18,200 entered, 5,900 reached the first email.

▸What to do

Between the address capture form on the site and the "Send Welcome 1" block, put a check that runs before the first send — syntax and domain existence; there is no such check in the flow today. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show the result: the Bounce Rate of the first email over the next full month against 4.95%.

For "Birthday" and "Address confirmation", check the address source before the first email the same way: the sources differ, the defect is the same.

03 the whole project · attribution Decide today

Subscription confirmations count as flow revenue

7.4% of flow revenue rests on transactional emailsAugust 2026: $312,400 of $4,210,000 and 48 orders out of 590; from the project's reporting data

The subscription confirmation email and the order messages arrive at the moment of purchase and intercept the revenue from the mailings that led to the purchase. By the platform's design such emails are excluded from attribution; on this project the exclusion is not configured, so the money figures of every other flow are understated by an unknown share.

▸What was checked

Three emails with a transactional profile carry attributed revenue for August: "Confirm your subscription" — $181,200, "Thank you for your order" — $80,100, "Order placed" — $51,100. For all three the revenue follows orders the customer is making at that moment anyway.

August 2026, project revenue by mailing type; the mailing profile is from the flow structure.

▸What is in the flow

"Order confirmation": a scheduled trigger, one group of steps, one mailing — "Confirm your subscription", transactional profile. The exit leads nowhere further: the flow ends with the email.

▸What to do

Configure the exclusion from attribution — for the three mailings or by a filter on the transactional type, so that new ones fall under it automatically; then recalculate attribution, and the revenue will redistribute to the remaining mailings. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show the result: the transactional line in flow revenue for the next month is zero.

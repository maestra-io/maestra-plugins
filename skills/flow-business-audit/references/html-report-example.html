Goal. The project has two goals carrying revenue in August; “Orders” was taken as the larger — a mechanical rule, not a judgement about what the project measures itself by. Under the other goal every money figure in this report would be different.

What the verdicts are measured against: the same month a year earlier. Nobody asked for a basis, so this is the one the audit falls back to — it is the safer of the two on scenarios that send in bursts. August against July is shown beside it throughout, as context.

These figures will not match the revenue you see elsewhere in the platform, and that is expected. Here every figure is counted on the date the message went out; the platform's business reports count the same money on the date the order was placed, so one order can fall in a different month on each. The comparison to make is between scenarios inside this report, not between this report and that screen.

Not available for this audit: the platform's own scenario report, and the email health benchmark. What that cost is in “What we do not know”.

Coverage: 60 of the 60 scenarios on the project's list were classified — 12 sent in the period, 1 is silent but still taking people in, 47 did not send. Scenarios present in the reporting data but missing from the list: 0.

Confidence: a confident verdict (“problem” or “fine”) on 3 of the 13 scenarios that got a row of their own with figures — the 12 that sent, plus the 1 that is silent and still taking people in; the remaining 10 are “can't judge”. The scenarios with a problem

Abandoned cart

Earns the most, and costs the base out of all proportion
Problem
What is observed
70 of the project's 114 unsubscribes for the month (61 %), from 15 % of its deliveries. August 2026, this scenario against the project as a whole over the same month: 0.4812 % against 0.1187 %, on a denominator of 14,550 — above the 1,000 threshold.
The cost: at the project's own rate this would be about 17 unsubscribes instead of 70 — roughly 53 contacts a month. Next step: look at which messages of the sequence produce them, message by message.
A year ago the rate was half of this — 0.2410 %. August 2025, same scenario. It was sending the same set of messages in both months, so the two are comparable.
This movement is its own, not the project's: over the same year the project went from 0.0850 % to 0.1187 % (+40 %), while this scenario doubled.
What is in the scenario
Five sends on one path, separated by waits of 1 hour, 1 day, 1 day, 2 days and 2 days; one gate before the first send — “Subscribed and valid” — and no re-check after any wait. The scenario's structure and its execution counts over the last thirty days (18 August – 17 September), not the audited month: 12,410 executions entered, 9,880 passed the gate, 9,865 reached the fifth send.
Read at full detail on the start block's neighbourhood: no limit on repeat entries per customer. Not read: the body of the gate's filter.
Why it moved
Two of the sequence's five messages produce 58 of the 70: the third (34) and the fifth (24). August 2026, this scenario broken down message by message — the same month and the same count as the figure above. The other three messages together produce 12.
This much is measured, not supposed.
Supposed: those two arrive too soon after the message before them. Not established — this is a supposition, and it is written as one.
What would settle it: whether the unsubscribes sit on the messages that arrive soonest after the previous one — the third and the fifth follow the one-day waits, the others two-day ones. Spread evenly across the sequence, this is not the reason.
What to fix
Lengthen the waits before the third and the fifth message from one day to two, and re-ask the gate “Subscribed and valid” after every wait. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show it worked: the unsubscribe share of the third and fifth messages over the next full month, against 0.4812 % now.

Birthday

15 % of the project's volume for 5.6 % of its revenue
Problem
What is observed
CTR 0.62 % against the project's 1.74 % — three times lower. August 2026, this scenario against the project as a whole over the same month; denominator 14,880 deliveries, above the threshold.
Against itself a year ago: 1.10 % → 0.62 % (−44 %), where the project moved −15 %. The movement is stronger than the project's, so it is this scenario's own.
There is no money verdict. 22 orders attributed to the goal “Orders” in August 2026, against a threshold of 50.
What would settle it: the same view over June–August, where the count reaches 64.
What is in the scenario
Starts on a schedule, daily, on customers whose birthday is in seven days; one condition — “Subscribed to email” — then one send, “Birthday greeting”, with no wait and no exit-time window. The scenario's structure and its execution counts over the last thirty days (18 August – 17 September), not the audited month: 15,120 executions entered, 14,900 passed the condition and reached the send.
Read at full detail on the send step's neighbourhood: the mailing is a marketing one, sent at the moment the schedule fires — 06:00 in the project's time zone.
Why it moved
Supposed: the message goes out at six in the morning, and a greeting read at breakfast is not clicked. Not established — the construction shows the hour; nothing in this run's figures ties the hour to the click share.
What would settle it: the click share of this message by hour of send over the next month, once a window is set — or against the project's other email sent in the morning.
What to fix
Put a wait with an exit window of 10:00–20:00 in the customer's time zone between the condition “Subscribed to email” and the send “Birthday greeting”. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show it worked: the click share of “Birthday greeting” over the next full month, against 0.62 % now.

What to do

Ordered by the money at stake where a finding has a figure for it, then by the number of people behind the finding — two of the four below have no revenue figure, so they are ordered on the second.

Actions
“Abandoned cart”: lengthen the waits before the third and the fifth message from one day to two, and re-ask the gate “Subscribed and valid” after every wait. For whoever runs the project in the platform. Those two messages produce 58 of the scenario's 70 unsubscribes for August 2026. What will show it worked: their unsubscribe share over the next full month, against 0.4812 % now.
“Birthday”: put a wait with an exit window of 10:00–20:00 in the customer's time zone between the condition “Subscribed to email” and the send “Birthday greeting”. For whoever runs the project in the platform. CTR 0.62 % against the project's 1.74 %, August 2026. What will show it worked: the message's click share over the next full month.
“Address confirmation”: look at what the condition “Address not yet confirmed” checks — it turns away all 2,040 executions that entered over the last thirty days — and whether the send step below it names a finished mailing. For whoever runs the project in the platform. A probe: the construction was read to the skeleton; the condition's body and the mailing's state were not.
Project-wide: set a frequency limit on the scenarios that share the email base — start with the three that grew most over the year. For whoever runs the project in the platform. Year on year: deliveries +37 %, opens −21 %, unsubscribes +40 %, bounces ×1.8. The limit's value is a decision for the project; what will show it worked: the project's unsubscribe share next month against 0.1187 %.

What we do not know

  • Per-customer figures are not available on this project. The scenario report returns customer counts as zero here while executions and every other figure are filled, so nothing in this audit is stated per unique customer — only per send, per delivery and per execution.
  • Unique reach into a scenario is not held in the reporting data — neither repeat entries nor distinct people. The one reach figure in this audit is the entry-point counter, and it covers its own rolling window of about 30 days, which is not the audit's period.
  • The project's other goals could not be listed. This audit counted money against the goal named in its header; nothing available to it enumerates the alternatives, so "was another goal worth counting" is a question for the project, not an omission of this report.
  • Per-scenario agreement with the platform's screens was not established. The project totals here stand up by method; an individual row of the table does not carry that guarantee.
  • August revenue is a lower bound. An order is attributed to a send for up to 30 days afterwards, so the −7 % against July may turn out to be zero, and the +17 % against August 2025 is “at least +17 %”. Volume comparisons are not affected.
  • How fresh the reporting data is could not be measured. Nothing available to this audit reports it, which is why a closed month was audited rather than a running one.
  • There is no industry benchmark in this audit. Every “fine” here is measured only against the project itself, and those verdicts are marked weak.
  • Five silent scenarios were checked — the limit of this audit. The remaining silent ones were not.
  • Why engagement is falling across the whole project is not established. It moves in the same direction on scenarios that have nothing to do with each other, which is what makes it one project-wide effect rather than several — but nothing here says what is behind it. The reading that would settle it is named beside that finding, and it is a question for your Maestra manager with those figures attached.
  • What the check after the entry of “Address confirmation” is testing is not visible in this audit. Where the 2,040 people stop is established; why that check turns them away is not.

01 “Abandoned cart” Decide today

The best-earning mechanic loses subscribers four times faster than the rest

0.48 % Unsubscribe Rate (Email) — against 0.12 % for the projectAugust 2026, 14,550 deliveries; from the project's reporting data
Unsubscribe Rate (Email) by month, September 2025 — August 2026 · from the project's reporting data

The scenario earns more than any other — $1,185,769.20 in August — and produces 70 of the project's 114 unsubscribes from 15 % of its deliveries. A year ago the share was half of this: 0.24 %. Over the same year the project grew 40 %, this scenario doubled.

▸Where exactly it is lost

Of the 70 unsubscribes, 58 come from two of the five messages: the third (34) and the fifth (24). The other three together produce 12.

August 2026, the scenario broken down message by message; the same month and the same goal as the figure on the slide.

▸What is in the scenario

Five sends on one path with waits of 1 hour, 1 day, 1 day, 2 days and 2 days. One gate before the first message — “Subscribed and valid” — and no re-check after any wait. The scenario has no limit on repeat entries.

The scenario's structure and its execution counts over the last thirty days (18 August — 17 September), not over August: 12,410 executions entered, 9,880 passed the gate, 9,865 reached the fifth message.

▸What to do

Lengthen the waits before the third and the fifth message from one day to two and re-ask the gate “Subscribed and valid” after every wait — for whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show it worked: the unsubscribe share of the third and fifth messages over the next full month, against 0.48 % now.

The hypothesis this tests: the unsubscribes sit on the messages that arrive soonest after the one before — the third and the fifth stand behind the one-day waits. If they spread evenly across the sequence, the waits are not the reason.

“Birthday” takes as many deliveries as the project's best-earning mechanic — 15 % — and returns 5.6 % of the revenue against its 56 %. It is clicked three times less often than the project's average message: 0.62 % against 1.74 %. A year ago it was 1.10 %: over that year the project lost 15 %, this scenario 44 %.

▸What is checked

A click share of 0.62 % against the project's 1.74 % over the same month, on a denominator of 14,880 deliveries, above the threshold. There is no money verdict: 22 orders against a threshold of 50; the same view over June — August would give one, where the count reaches 64.

August 2026, from the project's reporting data; the comparison with itself is August 2025.

▸What is in the scenario

Starts on a schedule, daily, on customers whose birthday is in seven days. One condition — “Subscribed to email” — then one send, “Birthday greeting”, with no wait and no exit-time window. The mailing is a marketing one and goes out the moment the schedule fires — at 06:00 in the project's time zone.

The scenario's structure and its execution counts over the last thirty days (18 August — 17 September), not over August: 15,120 executions entered, 14,900 passed the condition and reached the send.

▸What to do

Put a wait with an exit window of 10:00—20:00 in the customer's time zone between the condition “Subscribed to email” and the send “Birthday greeting”. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show it worked: the click share of “Birthday greeting” over the next full month, against 0.62 % now.

The hypothesis this tests: the message goes out at six in the morning, and a greeting read at breakfast is not clicked. If the click share does not move, the hour of the send is not the reason.

03 the whole project · contact frequency Idea

A third more messages went out this year, and noticeably fewer are read

+37 % deliveries over the year — while opens fell by 21 %August 2026 against August 2025, 96,500 deliveries; from the project's reporting data

Deliveries grew 37 % over the year, and every engagement figure worsened at once: opens −21 %, clicks −15 %, unsubscribes +40 %, bounces ×1.8. Almost every scenario moves the same way, so it is not about any one of them. Revenue per message over the same year fell from $25.50 to $21.81.

▸What this does not explain

On its own this does not say that there are too many messages: how many a single customer receives in a month is not held in the project's reporting data. That figure is the platform's own per-customer count, which is not populated on this project, so it comes from an export.

August 2026 against July 2026 and August 2025; from the project's reporting data.

▸What is in the scenario

Not one of the scenarios read here carries a contact-frequency limit: “Abandoned cart” has no limit on repeat entries, “Birthday” goes out on a schedule with no exit window, and both series write to the same email base.

The structure of the scenarios as read in this audit, and the execution counts over 18 August — 17 September.

▸What to do

Set a contact-frequency limit on the scenarios that share the email base — start with the three that grew most over the year. The value of the limit is a decision for the project. For whoever runs the project in the platform. What will show it worked: the project's unsubscribe share over the next month, against 0.1187 % now.

The hypothesis this tests: customers are receiving more messages than they used to. If the limit moves neither the unsubscribes nor the opens, the reason is elsewhere.
